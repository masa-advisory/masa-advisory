Linen creases. That is the point.

The fabric most men avoid is the one this city needs. How to wear it so the creases look intentional, not careless.

Masa Menswear Advisory  |  3 minute read

In brief
  • Linen breathes, dries fast and stands away from the skin, which is exactly what 84% humidity demands.
  • Soft creases at the elbow and lap read as natural fibre. Sharp fold lines and a crumpled back read as neglect.
  • Heavier linen, a relaxed fit and one crisp accessory turn creases into character.

Most men in Singapore own one linen shirt and wear it twice a year. The reason is always the same: it creases. That objection gets the fabric backwards. The creases are the visible sign of the property you want, a stiff natural fibre that does not cling to wet skin.

Why linen outperforms cotton here

Linen is spun from flax. The fibre is thicker and stiffer than cotton, so the cloth holds a little air between itself and your body instead of sticking to you. It absorbs moisture readily and releases it quickly, which means a linen shirt dries on the walk from the MRT while a cotton one stays damp until you reach the air-con.

Weave matters as much as fibre. A looser, more open linen weave lets air move through the cloth. Hold a shirt up to the light in the shop. If you can see a faint grid of light through it, it will wear cooler than one that looks solid.

Good creases and bad creases

Not all creases are equal, and this is where most men give up too early. Soft, rounded creases at the elbows, the inside of the arm and across the lap are the fabric doing its job. Nobody reads those as sloppy. They read as a man wearing real cloth.

Bad creases come from storage and sitting. Knife-sharp fold lines across the chest mean the shirt went straight from the drawer to your back. A crushed band across the lower back means it sat under you in a car for an hour. Both are easy to avoid.

A linen shirt should look lived in by the afternoon. It should never look slept in by the morning.

How to wear it so it looks deliberate

Choose a mid to heavy weight. Heavier linen drapes and creases in soft lines. Very light linen creases in small, sharp crinkles and turns see-through when you sweat.

Size for a slightly relaxed fit. Linen that is cut tight pulls into horizontal creases across the chest and back every time you move. Give it a little room through the body and it falls straight again when you stand.

Add one crisp element. Linen is relaxed by nature, so anchor it with something that holds its shape: suede loafers, a leather watch strap, a high-rise trouser with a clean waistband. The contrast is what makes the outfit look chosen rather than thrown on.

Stay in its colour range. White, ecru, sky blue, olive and navy all flatter linen's natural texture. Very saturated colours can look flat on it.

For the office, try a blend

If your workplace is more formal, a linen and cotton blend keeps most of the breathability and creases far less. It is the easiest first step for a man who wants to wear linen on a Tuesday, not just on holiday.

Care that takes five minutes

Wash cold, shake it out and hang it to dry on a proper hanger. Skip the tumble dryer. Steam or iron it once while slightly damp, then let it wear in. You are not trying to keep it flat all day. You are trying to start the day without fold lines.
