A knit polo does the job a blazer used to

Collar, texture and structure in one layer. What to look for in gauge, fibre and fit so it reads as considered, not casual.

Masa Menswear Advisory  |  3 minute read

In brief
  • A knit polo gives you a collar that frames the face and a textured surface, the two things a blazer usually adds.
  • Choose cotton or cotton-linen yarn in an open or fine knit. Avoid heavy synthetic blends in this heat.
  • Fit it at the shoulder bone and mid-bicep, with enough room that the knit never clings.

A blazer does two jobs for an outfit. The lapels frame the face, and the cloth adds texture and structure over a plain shirt. In Singapore, a wool blazer is too warm for most of the day. A knit polo does both jobs in a single layer.

Knit polo, not piqué polo

Most polos in Singapore are piqué: the sporty, mesh-textured shirt with a button placket and a soft collar that curls after a few washes. It reads as golf or weekend.

A knit polo is knitted like a fine sweater. The collar is usually knitted too, so it holds its shape and sits flat against the neck. Many have an open collar with no buttons, sometimes called a Johnny collar. The result looks closer to a shirt than to sportswear.

The collar is the lapel of the tropics. Choose one that holds its shape.

What to look for

Fibre first. Cotton and cotton-linen yarns breathe and handle sweat well. Silk blends feel cool and add a slight sheen for evenings. Be wary of polos that are mostly acrylic or polyester: they trap heat and pill quickly. Fine merino wool can work, but mainly for days spent in air-conditioned offices.

Then the knit. Gauge describes how fine the knit is. A finer gauge looks smoother and dressier, good for the office. An open, more visible knit looks relaxed and lets more air through, good for weekends. Hold it up to the light: if you can see through the stitch, it will wear cool.

Then the collar. Check it holds its shape on the hanger. A collar that flops in the shop will flop by noon.

How it should fit

The shoulder seam should sit on your shoulder bone, not hang past it. Sleeves should end around mid-bicep. The body should skim, not grip. Knits stretch and cling as you sweat, so a polo that is tight in the fitting room will look tighter outside.

Length depends on how you wear it. To tuck, you need enough length that it stays in when you sit. To wear it out, the hem should land around the middle of your trouser fly.

Two ways to wear it

For the office, tuck a fine-gauge polo in navy, black or brown into high-rise pleated trousers and add suede loafers. It holds its own in a client meeting without a jacket.

For the weekend, wear an open-knit polo in cream, sage or black untucked over linen shorts or drawstring trousers, with sandals. Same garment, different register.

Care

Wash cold, inside out, on a gentle cycle. Dry it flat. Never hang a wet knit: the weight of the water stretches it out of shape. Fold it for storage instead of hanging.
