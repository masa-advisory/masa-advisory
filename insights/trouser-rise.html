Most men in Singapore wear their trousers too low

A low rise shortens the leg and untucks your shirt by lunch. On the days you skip the jacket, the waist decides the whole outfit.

Masa Menswear Advisory  |  3 minute read

In brief
  • Rise is the distance from the crotch seam to the top of the waistband. It sets where your leg visually begins.
  • Without a jacket, the waistband becomes the centre of the outfit. A low rise puts it in the wrong place.
  • Aim for a waistband at or just below the navel, then adjust for your torso length.

Walk through any mall in Singapore and most trousers on the rack sit low on the hip. That cut came from jeans, where a low waist was the fashion for two decades. It carried over into chinos and office trousers, and most men never questioned it.

In a colder city, a jacket hides the problem. It covers the waist and draws its own line across the body. Here, you are in a shirt or a polo most days. With nothing on top, the waistband is the strongest horizontal line in the outfit, and a low one works against you.

What a low rise does

It shortens the leg. The eye reads the leg as starting where the trouser starts. Drop the waistband to the hip and the torso looks longer while the legs look shorter.

It untucks the shirt. A low waistband sits below the widest part of the hip, so every time you sit, reach or climb stairs, the shirt pulls out. By lunch it is half out at the back.

It breaks the line at the waist. A low rise leaves fabric bunching above the belt and a visible gap when you lean forward. None of this shows under a blazer. All of it shows in a tucked shirt.

On most days in Singapore, the waistband does the job the jacket does elsewhere. Put it where it frames you.

Finding the right rise for your frame

Start at the natural waist, which sits at or just below the navel. That is where a high-rise trouser is designed to sit, and where most classic tailoring placed the waistband before the low-rise era.

Then adjust for your proportions. A man with a longer torso and shorter legs gains the most from a high rise, because it lifts the start of the leg. A man with a shorter torso may prefer a mid-high rise so the trouser does not swallow his upper body. This is one reason copying a trend photo rarely works: the model's proportions are not yours.

Do the sitting test in the fitting room. Tuck your shirt in, sit down, lean forward and stand up again. If the shirt stays in and the waistband does not dig, the rise is right.

Details that help in the heat

Side adjusters instead of belt loops give a cleaner waist with less bulk. A single forward or reverse pleat adds room through the hip and thigh, so the cloth moves away from your skin when you sit in humidity. Choose linen, cotton or a lightweight wool for the cloth itself.

Where to start

High-rise ready-to-wear trousers exist, but they are harder to find in Singapore malls than low-rise ones. A made-to-measure tailor is often the most reliable route. Ask for a high rise by name and say where you want the waistband to sit. A good tailor will know exactly what you mean.
