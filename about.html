About

Japanese restraint, Neapolitan cloth, Singapore heat.

Masa was started by a Japanese-Singaporean who has spent years dressing for this climate and got tired of style advice written for somewhere else. In Japanese, masa can be written 雅, a character for elegance and refinement. It is the standard every outfit is held to.

Two traditions shape the eye. From Japan: restraint, precision and ma (間), the idea that the space between things is part of the design. A garden, a room or an outfit is judged by what is left out. It is why we edit an outfit down to what earns its place. From Naples: soft shoulders, open weaves and cloth built for southern Italian summers.

The method is local: what works on a humid Tuesday in Raffles Place, a Saturday on Keong Saik Road and a Sunday lunch in Joo Chiat.

We take a small number of clients at a time, so every wardrobe gets proper attention.

Our point of view

Most style advice is written for cold cities. In Singapore, the cloth decides.

Most menswear advice is written in London, New York and Milan, for four seasons. There, a shirt, a wool knit and a structured jacket trap warm air for a reason. Borrow that formula at 84% humidity and the outfit fails before you reach the office lift.

So men here pick one of two options. Some copy the look and suffer through it. Most give up and live in a T-shirt and shorts. Neither is necessary.

An outfit gets depth from texture, proportion and colour, not from piece count. A knit polo with a collar that holds its shape, a high-rise pleated trouser and a suede loafer reads as deliberate in a client meeting, and it survives the walk from the MRT.

When the occasion calls for a jacket, make it unstructured linen: soft shoulders, little or no lining, cloth that lets air through. It adds polish without adding heat.

Piece count is not style. Fit, fabric and proportion are.
